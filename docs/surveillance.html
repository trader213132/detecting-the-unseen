      The difference is in the trader's head. The spoof's order was never meant to trade; the honest one was, until the news changed. Nothing in the order data records that. This is why an alert is a reason to look closer, not proof.

      From a pattern to a payoff

      Detecting a pattern and knowing whether it pays are different questions. The Economics proposal is about the second one.

      01Measured · simulation

      A fake order changes what others do

      With a large fake buy order on the book, the trader's genuine sell order filled 81% of the time. In the matched honest run, without it, 69%.

      +0 points

      95% interval +6 to +20 points · 8× wall, calm market · results/summary.md

      02Development data only

      An effect is not a profit

      The Break-Even pilot subtracts the cost of the fake order itself being traded by accident. Across 48 simulated markets, the net payoff's 95% interval sat above zero in 5, all with 16× walls and calm or very responsive books. In volatile markets the fake order was often traded by accident: 18 of the 24 volatile cells were clearly negative.

      With 48 intervals, about one could sit above zero by chance. The test on untouched seeds has not been run.

      03Measured · simulation

      A suspicious pattern is not proof

      The law-informed detector flags 93% of 16× spoofs. It also flags 92% of honest traders who changed their mind. Asked to tell the two apart, it scores 0.50: a coin toss.

      AUC 0.00

      95% interval 0.46 to 0.55 · results/teaching.md

      04Proposed · not yet run

      So: when does it pay, and what should deter it?

      Becker (1968): a rational trader weighs the gain against the chance of a penalty times its size. My model: expected extra payoff = G − U − C − pF.

      In my pilot, G and U are measured in lots, C is zero (the simulator has no fees), p is my detector's alert rate, and F is a scenario, not a real fine. Raising p with this detector also raises false alarms on honest traders, and that cost belongs in the model too. Testing this properly is the Sixth Form project.
